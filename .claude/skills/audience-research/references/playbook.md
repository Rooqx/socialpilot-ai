# Audience evidence matrix

## Distinguish observed language from marketing assumptions

A useful audience finding consists of a **question/problem**, an **exact source**, **date**, **context**, **who seems to be asking**, and **what the evidence does not prove**. Do not present three public comments as a validated market segment. When sampling from Reddit, X, LinkedIn, creator comments or review sites, respect permitted access and avoid saving unnecessary personally identifying text.

## Research workflow

1. Define the decision the research must improve: topic selection, depth, tone, platform choice or format. Set a bounded sampling plan.
2. Create source buckets: existing customer/user questions, public discussion, interviews, support requests, search intent and competing material. Record how many observations came from each.
3. Code evidence into themes: desired outcome, confusion, repeated objection, technical level, exact language, and unanswered question. Keep dissenting examples.
4. Distinguish **frequency in sample** from population frequency. Identify geographic, time, self-selection and platform biases.
5. Rank opportunities by evidence strength and creator credibility—not hypothetical virality. Provide a relevant post angle and the proof required to publish it.
6. Save dated findings; expire fast-moving language and trends rather than ossifying them into permanent brand facts.

## Example

Four public questions ask how to handle failed jobs in a Node queue. Valid conclusion: *this sample shows repeated interest in retry behavior*. Invalid conclusion: *80% of developers struggle with retries*. Useful angles: "the three failure states worth logging" and "when retries increase damage." Any asserted prevalence requires a real representative data source.

## Acceptance checklist

Every theme points to evidence; hypotheses are separated from facts; minority/counterexamples are preserved; no private account content was silently scraped; proposed content answers a real question; the researcher states where the sample was thin or stale.
