# From observation to publishable content brief

## Score substance before novelty

Maintain source lineage from every idea: `origin`, `received_at`, `central_claim`, `target_reader`, `reader_payoff`, `proposed_format`, `proof_available`, `new_information_needed`, and `family_key`. Rank qualitatively by usefulness, specificity, author credibility, freshness, production cost and duplication risk. Never assign a numeric virality probability.

## Expansion and selection

1. Start from one seed: a user question, bug fix, source fact, comment, customer conversation or research result.
2. Generate angles with *different promises*, not five synonyms. Examples: explain the failure, demonstrate a fix, contrast valid choices, expose a tradeoff, or ask a genuinely unresolved question.
3. Write the one-sentence thesis for each and identify the evidence needed to make it true.
4. Check calendar, approved posts and published history for **semantic** duplicates: identical central lesson under different wording is still duplicate.
5. Prioritize one default angle and one alternative; explain why it fits current audience and platform.
6. Assign stable keys. Unsupported personal-case-study angles become `needs_creator_input`, not fabricated stories.

## Worked seed

Seed: "Spent the afternoon debugging a Docker deployment" (creator-supplied but without detail). Angle A: a personal lesson **requires** error and fix confirmation. Angle B: a general checklist can be drafted as *advice* with separately verified steps. Angle C: a comparison of two deployment strategies requires actual tradeoff sources. Do not turn the seed into "how I saved our startup $50,000".

## Anti-duplication quality check

Two ideas are duplicates if the reader would leave with the same actionable insight despite different hooks. A carousel and X thread can share a content-family key while using distinct platform-specific formats. Record reused assets, not just word similarity.
