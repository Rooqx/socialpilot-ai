# Strategy as a falsifiable operating plan

## Start from outcome and credibility

Define the purpose of posting, target reader, what makes the creator qualified to speak, and the capacity to create useful work. Do not default to five posts weekly or a universal content mix. A strong pillar is an intersection of a real audience question, authentic proof and an angle the creator can revisit without repeating themselves.

## Build the strategy

1. Translate objectives into observable signals: helpful replies, qualified conversations, portfolio inquiries, clicks with provenance, saves or learning outcomes. Distinguish vanity measures from business outcomes.
2. Define 3–5 proposed pillars. Give each one a reader problem, core promise, credibility source, typical formats, and exclusion rules.
3. Create a **pillar boundary test**: could the proposed post belong to two pillars? If yes, simplify or specify how to classify it.
4. Select platforms based on where target readers actually engage, creative capacity and suitable forms. Explain why the second platform receives adaptations rather than a duplicate feed.
5. Set a feasible review cadence and content capacity, with space for genuine new developments. Get approval on pillars rather than silently treating them as final.
6. Define two or three small experiments that change one meaningful element while holding other conditions as stable as possible.

## Example pillar card

**Pillar:** Backend reliability. **Reader:** early-career developers. **Reader question:** why does code that works locally break after deployment? **Credible source:** user's actual checked deployment logs and project lessons. **Post forms:** X single insight, LinkedIn postmortem, Instagram error-to-fix carousel. **Do not:** invent incident frequency, infrastructure size or a real company customer impact.

## Revision triggers

Review after an explicit change in goals, platform capability, verified audience needs or a sufficiently repeated analytics pattern. Keep a strategy decision log with owner approval. One unusually popular post is a hypothesis generator, not permission to discard the plan.
